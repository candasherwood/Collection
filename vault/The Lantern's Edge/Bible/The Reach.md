# The Reach

*The world of [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]]: a ring that broke, and the
people who have been living on the break for four hundred years.*

## Shape of the world

The Reach is the inhabited rim of the **Circlet** — a ring the size of a world,
broken a long time ago by something nobody agrees about. Two thousand miles of
coast, cliffs, fishing towns and nine lantern-keeps, curving gently out of sight
of itself. Behind the towns: moor, salt marsh, and the old machine-country, where
the ring's bones come out of the ground in ribs of grey metal.

At both ends of the arc the sea simply stops at the **Fall**: water going over an
edge into nothing, with a noise you feel in your teeth from thirty miles off. No
one has ever come back from over the Fall to say what is down there — or one man
did, eleven years ago, and was hanged before anyone could make him prove it. The
Brine Mother is generally assumed to know.

Above, on a clear night, the other side of the ring: a band of dark metal
crossing the sky, studded with dead lights. Every child learns the same rhyme
about it and no child finds it comforting.

The dark matters more than the map. Between the lanterns there is **dark water**:
no stars, no wind, ships that drift with no one aboard. Sailing it takes a
navigator who can see the un-ships, or a captain with a debt large enough to
outweigh her sense.

## The nine lanterns

Numbered outward from the Kindling, where the Emberwatch's forge-fortress
stands. Eight of the nine were doused eleven years ago; **five have been relit under Crown licence
since**, which is the arrangement the Crown has been charging rent for. At the start of the book
the relit five are the Fifth at Saltmere, the Sixth at the Gull's Teeth, and three keeps the text
never names, and the Ninth is dark.

## Powers

**Thursday at the Kindling (Ch. 24).** The writ is served in person: the **forge sealed** for
nine days with the Crown's seal beside the Warden's, a Crown smith in the antechamber, no cutting
and no quenching; the **guides and the drowned left off the Glass Roll** for nine days; the
Ninth's Ember taken into the *Surety*'s strongroom as Crown property, logged and **unassessed**;
and Mira Calloway on a **warrant of sufferance** that dies with the term. The Ash's window comes
in from nine miles to five the same evening, because a light went up in the roads and the dark
noticed.

**The Salt Throne.** The crown at Saltmere. Eleven years ago it ordered eight of
the nine lanterns put out, on the argument that light is a beacon and dark is a
harbour. The instrument is real and it is four lines long: eight lights withdrawn *for a term
of eleven years and a day*, in consideration whereof the Crown undertook to keep the coast by
ships, guns and the diligence of its officers, the term to end on **the ninth night of the
ninth month** — restore the lights, renew the covenant, or *otherwise determine* (Ch. 23).
The Crown has sent four ships in eleven years: a survey, a transport of auditors, and two
wrecks. The same night is the Brine Mother's deadline, which nobody at Saltmere has noticed.
 The order worked: the Ash stopped coming ashore. It also took the roads,
the safe shipping, the histories and the coastal towns that depended on them, and
the Crown has been quietly charging rent for the dark ever since.

**The Emberwatch.** The order of knight-keepers. Vows of poverty, obedience and
attendance; a blade, an iron thumb-ring, no coin in the purse and the safest
signature on the coast. Founded to keep the nine lit. For eleven years it has
followed Crown instruction and called it obedience, and the knights who notice are
quietly sent on long errands. Ser Cadan Rook is one of them. Full codex:
[[The Emberwatch]].

**The free crews.** Smugglers, wreckers, salvagers and pirates who live by the
dark water and know exactly what it costs. The Crown calls them criminals; the
coast calls them the post. Captain Isolde Sable is the best of them and the most
indebted.

**The Scarlet Assize.** The order's first heresy, two hundred years out in the machine-country:
about forty men and women who quench **red blades** in seasons taken, hold that the coast has been
kept soft by a light it did not pay for, and come to a coast the way a smith comes to a blade —
to temper it. They sail a black ship with three red lights at the masthead and **the Ash runs from
them**, because a red blade is a door nailed shut. Ordinator **Anselm Cray** brings twelve of them
up the Kindling's slip in Ch. 25, cuts the nine-slot door with a single pass, and leaves a red
blade burning in the stair. Full codex: [[The Scarlet Assize]].

**The Brine Mother.** The deep. Not a god — a creditor. She raises hulls, keeps
bargains, and collects in units of light. Her agents are the drowned, the ropes
that move by themselves, and a woman at the foot of the Fall who has never
once blinked.

**The Receiver (Ch. 28).** That woman: about forty, grey coat, a book with a clasp, and she walks
out of the middle of the bay to the shingle without her feet going under the water. She holds the Mother's book
for this coast and has held it four hundred years, and she is a clerk — *I do not choose. The Mother
is the creditor.* The account reads: eleven years of light at nine lanterns, owed, **payable in
light or in what light has taken**; and the collection, when it comes, is paid in people, one at a
time, over the lip. She cannot be fought, argued with, or hurried, and nothing in the book is
personal. That is what makes her frightening.

**The Ash.** See [[The Light of Will]]. It owns everything east of the Eighth and
it is coming west.

## The Last Word and the Long Hunt

See [[The Vow and the Last Word]] for the text. In the world, it is this:

Eleven years ago the Crown ordered the eight outer lanterns put out. In the same
week a free captain named Alder Vane took a lit lamp out past the Ninth and came
back saying he had been over the Fall and back. They hanged him at Saltmere for
*bearing a light against the Throne*, in front of four thousand people, and asked
him if he had anything to say, and he said it.

- **The Vane Statute** makes repeating the words in a public house a forty-scrip
  fine. It is the most widely broken law on the Reach.
- **The Long Hunt** is the loose, permanent, badly organised fleet of crews who
  sail for the Fall looking for what he left. Some come back with salvage, some
  with debts, some not at all. The Crown calls them wreckers and taxes them
  anyway.
- **The proverb** — *that's the whole map* — means a plan held together by nerve
  and nothing else. It is said admiringly and it is said as an insult.
- **The name** is common. Half the boys on the Saltmere docks were named Vane in
  the year after the hanging, from the story, including one left on a pier and
  taken aboard a lugger that should have been at the bottom of the sea.

## Ships and the sea

- **The *Mercy Bell*** — two-masted lugger, ninety feet, black hull, a bronze bell
  on the foredeck that rings in still air. Raised from the bottom by the Brine
  Mother with her crew still aboard. She runs dark water for hire.
- **The crew:** Isolde Sable (captain), Teodor Vane (navigator), Marrow (bosun,
  too old, too strong, cannot drown, remembers nothing before the water), Nettle
  (surgeon, keeps a jar of teeth for reasons), Pell (cook, sixteen, terrified,
  indispensable). Six hands and a goat nobody will admit to owning.
- **The Crown's *Surety*** — the Admiral's new three-decker, black and long, glass-sight masthead
  and not one lantern along ninety yards of deck, because Voss does not like what light shows her.
  She lands her writ from it at the Kindling in Ch. 24. Her old *Vigil* is the Ash's ship now, so
  the fleet she brings to the Kindling is nine sail: the *Surety*, a transport, two cutters, and
  five of what a clerk calls vessels.
- **The Crown's *Vigil*** — a three-decker with a glass-sight masthead and no
  lantern, because the Admiral does not like what light shows her. Taken at anchor off
  Barrowfoot without a blow struck and sailed out past the Cradle by a crew of the
  Hollowed, handling her perfectly, with one window lit aft (Ch. 19).
- **The Un-ships** — before-Fall vessels, whole fleets of them, sailing the dark
  water with nobody at the wheel, visible only in lantern light. Counting them is
  a commoner's superstition and an admiral's intelligence report.

Sea weather as Ash symptom: **the Quiet** (flat water, no sound, no wind), salt
fog that tastes of iron, and bells at the wrong hour.

## The marked and the Roll

A minority of the coast is changed by the Fall and by looking at it too long: the
glass-eyed who see without a lamp, the drowned who do not stay down, the Bright who
light without a wick, the hollow-touched who can pass among the Hollowed. The Crown
keeps their names in the **Glass Roll**, and the Emberwatch keeps the Roll, and calls
it charity. Full detail: [[The Marked]].

## Daily life

Late-medieval with relics: crossbows, cutlasses, hooks, one thunderer cannon per
ship, oil lamps, hand-copied books. Ember-tech is expensive and mostly in the
hands of the order and the Crown — glass-sights, ember-blades, the odd pump that
runs on a splinter of Ember and mutters at night.

Money is salt-scrip. News travels by ship or not at all. Most people on the Reach
have never seen a lit lantern and do not believe the stories about the road under
the water, which is convenient for everyone except the people who need the road.

## Places to keep straight

- **Carrow** — Mira's island. Four hundred people, one hill, one dark tower, four
  hundred yards of sea-wall, one chandlery, and a parish that has decided a woman
  walking the wall every night for eleven years is *mad* rather than *the defence of the
  coast*. One grandmother-shaped hole in the local memory.
- **Saltmere** — the capital. The Throne, the admiralty, the chandelier that used
  to be the Fourth Lantern's lens.
- **The Kindling** — fortress-forge, the order's heart, a furnace that has been burning for
  four hundred years on a shard of the old sun. The drowned road goes *under* it and comes up
  a stair of three hundred steps into the cellars, through an iron door with nine slots and
  eight pins; the ninth pin is in the Warden's drawer. The guides work out of a cellar, and
  one of the nine doors of the Reach is that door ([[The Bands]]).
- **The Drowned Road** — the pre-Fall coast road. Walkable at low light.
- **The Fall** — the edge. Sea going over. Don't.
