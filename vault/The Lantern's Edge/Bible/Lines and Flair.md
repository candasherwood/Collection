# Lines and Flair

*The three registers [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]] actually speaks in, with
sample lines. Steal the cadence, never the catchphrase.*

## Register one — the rogue (Isolde, and the crew when she is not listening)

The pirate-captain voice is a **performance with a real bill at the end**. She
answers the question she would rather have been asked. She turns dignity into a
joke and a joke into a threat, in that order, in one breath. She never lies about
anything that matters and she never once tells the whole truth about anything at
all.

Rules:

- **Misdirection, not bluffing.** She cannot lie in lantern light and knows it, so
  she tells the truth in the wrong order instead. "I didn't get past the ones who
  were looking."
- **The title is armour.** Captain is the only rank on the Reach you can buy, and
  she bought it twice. Running joke: she corrects people, and it is funny, and it
  stops being funny in Part Five when nobody has to be reminded.
- **Hands full.** She talks while eating, splicing, counting coin, cleaning a
  knife. The props do the punctuation.
- **Jokes at funerals, never at the frightened.** Her humour is aimed at power,
  death and herself. Never at Pell.

Usable lines, in escalating order:

1. "Isolde Sable. That is *Captain* Isolde Sable, of the *Mercy Bell* — and you
   are Ede Calloway's girl, which is a coincidence I fully intend to profit from."
2. "I've been called worse by better, and paid by both."
3. "Stealing is an ugly word. I prefer *recovering*, and I charge for it."
4. "I'm a great believer in other people's honour. It's cheaper than mine."
5. "There's no such thing as a fair trade at sea. There's a trade you can live
   with, and a trade you can't."
6. "If this goes wrong — and it will — the plan is to be somewhere else, loudly."
7. "I have been dead before. I don't recommend the paperwork."

Do not: slur, stagger, say *savvy*, or make rum a personality. Sparrow's engine is
that he is the most competent person in the room and the most frightened, and the
jokes are the load-bearing wall between those two facts.

## Register two — the oath (Rook, the Warden, anyone under a vow)

High-fantasy formality with the archaism taken out. No *thee*, no *mayhap*, no
"so it begins". The weight comes from plain words in the right order and from a
man saying exactly what he means at cost.

Rules:

- **Speaks in futures.** "I will." Never "yes".
- **The more it costs him, the more formal he becomes.** That is the tell.
- **Never a speech unless the room is silent.** Rook's best line in the book is
  four words long.

Usable lines:

- "The light is not ours to keep. It is ours to hold, and to hand on."
- "I have given my word. It is a poor thing, but it is entirely mine."
- "Then I will be the last to leave."
- "You are asking me to choose between my oath and my duty. I had not noticed
  they were different."
- "I will not be the man who did this twice."

## Register three — the land (narration)

Elegiac. Deep time, small people, a world that was old before anyone started
keeping records and will be old after the last name is forgotten. The sea does not
care; the light does; that is the whole argument.

Rules:

- **Age is felt, not stated.** "Before the Fall." "The year the light went out."
  Never a date the reader has to convert.
- **Landscape gets a vote.** Weather, tide, cold, the smell of iron in fog.
- **Use *still* and *yet* for endurance — twice a chapter, not twice a page.**
- **No wink.** The narrator never knows more than Mira and never enjoys itself.
- **Close on a short, flat sentence.** The elegy lands on the plain thing.

Usable lines:

- "The fleet was gone. Not gone — unwitnessed."
- "It was not a fleet. It was a country that had not been told."
- "The sea was very old, and did not mind."
- "Old things do not hate you. They simply go on."
- "The tower stood all night with its lamp dark, the way a house stands after
  everyone has gone."
- "There are two kinds of courage: the one that lights the lamp, and the one that
  stays beside it."
- "It is a small thing to hold a door. It is not a small thing to hold it all
  night."

## Where they go

| Register | Chapters |
| --- | --- |
| Rogue | 4, 6, 9, 13, 25 — and never after 27. In Part Five she stops performing, and everyone notices |
| Oath | 5, 14, 22, 23, 26, 27 |
| Land | Chapter openings, the last paragraph of each part, and the last line of the book |

**Two flair lines per chapter, maximum.** Beyond that the book stops being a novel
and becomes a quotation anthology, which is the only real danger in this whole
influences list.
