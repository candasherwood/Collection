# The Ladder

*How power grows in [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]], what it costs at every
rung, and why nobody in the book ever says the word "level".*

## The principle

The light gives back exactly what you can picture and hold. So growth is not more
magic. Growth is a **wider gap between the keeper and the source** — distance, and
duration, and how many people you can carry across that gap.

That is the whole ladder. Every rung is the same trick done further away, and every
rung takes more than the one before.

## The five rungs

| Rung | Name | What she can do | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Wick** | Hold a shape in sight, touching the light. A rope, a hook, a door | A face or a name |
| 2 | **Hand** | Hold a shape she cannot see, at a distance, for a count of ninety | A sound |
| 3 | **Voice** | Send the light out as a road or a beacon; be seen at range; see through it | A smell, and a name |
| 4 | **Road** | Hold a light with no flame behind it — no wick, no lamp, only will | A season |
| 5 | **Horizon** | Be the light. Hold a line the width of a coast | The thing she is: the vow, and the joining-up of the words she has been saying since she was six |

The price escalates in kind, not just in size: **face → sound → smell and name →
season → the vow.** The last one is not death and it is not nothing: it is the one
thing she was good at, and Chapter Twenty-Nine takes it with nine miles of wall in
her hands. Anyone who tells you the top rung costs your life has not read the bill. She never gets to choose what goes. The one thing she would have
picked is never the thing taken, which is the cruelty and the engine both.

## Rules

1. **A rung is earned by a choice in a scene**, never by wanting it, never by
   practice, never by a montage. She does something she could not have done last
   week because somebody needed it, and the ladder notices.
2. **The price is paid in the same scene** and understood later. The reader should
   feel it land before she does.
3. **One rung a chapter, at most.** Usually one a part.
4. **Growth never looks like a glow.** It looks like *what she can now do from
   further away*, and like the people around her becoming frightened of her.
5. **A rung does not solve the theme.** The light can hold a door; it cannot make
   anyone forgive anybody.
6. **Rung 5 does not come back down.** What is spent at the Fall is not recovered in
   this book: Chapter Thirty is written by a keeper who cannot say her own short
   form and has to be told it. Whatever she gets back in book two, she gets back by
   earning it a second time, not by the bill being reversed.

## The coal

In Chapter Eleven, Alys Wren pours the last of herself into Mira's ring: a **coal**, a
small steady weight of light with no wick, no oil and no lamp behind it.

- It is not stored light. A ring cannot store light (see [[The Light of Will]]). It is
a *seed of will* — somebody else's conviction, still burning, that remembers how to
be a flame.
- Mira cannot use it. Rung four is the ability to hold a light with no flame, and she
is at rung two. In Part Three she learns to feed it; in Part Four it does what she
asks.
- It costs nothing to carry and everything to spend: the first time she uses it, she
pays a season rather than a sound.
- It is also the book's one inheritance that is not blood. Nobody chose Mira. A dying
woman handed her a coal because she was standing there and did not look away.
- It is spent in Chapter Twelve on the toll ([[The Toll]]), and the ring is cold
  afterwards. She does not get another one. Everything after that she has to build.

## Everybody climbs something

Mira's is the ladder. Everyone else has a side-ladder with its own price, and the
book should treat them exactly as seriously.

- **Teo — sight.** Rung 1: sees the un-ships. Rung 2: sees where the Ash *ends*.
  Rung 3: can steer by it in a dead calm. Price: sleep first, then the ability to
  be certain of anything he has not seen with his own eyes.
- **Rook — the blade.** It lights when he means it. Rung 2: it stays lit when he is
  exhausted. Rung 3: it lights *for someone else's* conviction, which nearly breaks
  him. His top rung is a sacrifice, not a triumph: the blade goes out in Chapter
  Twenty-Six and he holds the line anyway.
- **Alys — already at the top.** She reached Rung 4 without a wick and has been
  paying a season a year for eleven years. She is the warning the ladder was
  supposed to give and nobody read.
- **Ede — Rung 4, to the day.** She holds a ring round Carrow at the waterline with no
  colour in it at all (Ch. 20), one season a year, and has nothing left to spend. She is
  what the ladder looks like when somebody climbs it quietly, in a coat, for eleven
  years, and nobody ever comes to look. The difference between Ede and Alys is only that
  Ede had arithmetic and a hoop and Alys had a fire; the bill is identical.
- **Sallow — the drowned ladder.** Cannot die. Each death costs him the memory of
  dying, so he no longer knows how many times it has happened. He has stopped
  counting, which he says is a professional decision.
- **Marrow — no ladder.** He is already at the bottom of his, which is the joke and
  the grief: the strongest man aboard has nowhere to climb to.

## What to avoid

- No training montage, no "she felt a surge of power", no internal power-level
  narration. Mira experiences the ladder as *arithmetic and fear*, not as magic.
- Never use a rung to win a fight. Use it to keep something lit while somebody else
  does the human thing.
- Never let a rung make her less tired, less hungry, or less likely to be wrong.
- No prophecy, no inheritance, no bloodline. She is not chosen; she is *practised*,
  and the thing she practises is not being afraid in public.
