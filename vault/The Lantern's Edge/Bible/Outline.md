# Outline

*Structure for [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]]. Five parts, thirty chapters,
124,700 words. Chapters already written are marked ✔.*

See also: [[Characters]], [[The Reach]], [[The Light of Will]],
[[Tone and Influences]].

## Logline

A girl relights the last dark tower on the edge of a broken ring, and every power
on the coast — a crown that sold the dark, a knight sworn to keep it, a captain
who owes the sea a light — arrives within the week to take it from her.

## The five parts

**One — The Ninth Light (Ch. 1–7).** Carrow. The tower, the notebook, the lamp.
The un-ships. Teo, Isolde, Rook. The first Holding. They leave with the Ember and
a knight who should have stopped them.
*Turn:* Mira learns the light costs memory, and pays without being asked.

**Two — The Mercy Bell (Ch. 8–14).** Dark water. The debt to the Brine Mother and
what she actually wants. The Eighth lantern at Wren's Fold: a family keep, three
generations Hollowed at their posts, the wick still trimmed. Rook aboard, hands
tied, being slowly converted by dinner.
*Turn:* the Crown did not lose the outer lanterns — it put them out, and the
Admiral was right about why.

**Three — The Drowned Road (Ch. 15–21).** The old coast road under three feet of
water, walkable only in lantern light, and the only route to the Kindling before
the *Vigil*. Isolde's debt comes due. Teo's lies fail in the light. Voss's fleet
arrives at the Seventh and finds the Hollow Bell ringing.
*Turn:* Ede is alive, holding a ring round Carrow with no light in it at all, and
she has been writing her granddaughter's name at the top of a page every morning
for eleven years without knowing whose it is. And the Ash is not besieging the wall:
it is following the light, and the light walks north out of Carrow at the end of the
part with a three-decker behind it.

**Four — The Kindling (Ch. 22–27).** The order's fortress-forge. The Warden, the
vows, the truth of the Long Dark: the crown bought eleven years of safety with
eight lanterns and the Reach's memory. Rook chooses. Isolde sells the Ember and
buys it back with the *Mercy Bell*.
*Turn:* Mira decides to light the ring — all of it — knowing exactly what it will
cost her.

**Five — The Edge (Ch. 28–30).** The Fall. The Ash comes ashore at last because
it is invited to. Mira holds a light the size of a horizon and forgets almost
everything. Rook holds the line. Isolde pays her debt in the only currency she
has left. Ede gets one sentence. Teo, who has been writing everything down since
Chapter Seven, reads Mira back to herself on the last page.

**Ending image:** the Ninth is lit, the road is gold, the un-ships are sailing,
and two people sit on a cold deck with a notebook between them, deciding what to
remember on purpose.

## Chapter beats

### Part One

1. ✔ **Chapter One** — Carrow, the cliff path, the dark tower, page nineteen.
2. ✔ **Chapter Two** — one hundred and twelve steps; the lamp room is warm; the
   sea goes quiet.
3. ✔ **Chapter Three** — the matches; the vow said wrong; the log's five hundred
   and seventy weeks of *trimmed and filled* (the book's first sight of Ede's
   silence; Ch. 21's notebook calls the count back); the lamp takes; the sea fills with
   ships that are not there; the bell of a lugger; a stranger on the stairs.
4. ✔ **Chapter Four** — the parish will come up to look and not to help; the log
   read by a pirate (*that's a penance, somebody's been keeping a promise at you*);
   the light as a toll; Teo, then Isolde: the offer, the debt, the refusal. The
   *Vigil* on the horizon. Rook at the door.
5. ✔ **Chapter Five** — the dousing order; the Hollowed boat crew; Mira's first
   Holding; the cost (her grandmother's face); Isolde cuts the Ember free; escape
   in the dark; the name in the notebook.
6. ✔ **Chapter Six** — the *Mercy Bell* at sea. The crew, the rules, the goat
   nobody owns. The knife and the biscuit. At dusk, at the bow, the two of them
   say the counter-vow: *if I forget you* / *then I'll introduce myself again*
   ([[The Two of Them]]). Rook arrives by stolen boat, because the writ does not
   specify whose ship.
7. ✔ **Chapter Seven** — the storm that isn't weather (the Quiet); Teo steers by
   un-ships; Mira holds the mast. Rook in the dark of the hold, explaining what a
   poor knight owns and the three questions of the Long Night
   ([[The Emberwatch]]). Isolde names her price: stand before the Brine Mother
   and say no. Ends on a lit Eighth.

### Part Two

8. ✔ **Chapter Eight** — Wren's Fold: three generations at their posts, Hollowed,
   the wick still trimmed. The horror is the tidiness. Alys Wren, a Bright, has
   been holding the light out of her own will for eleven years and is nearly out;
   the Roll says *assessment deferred*, in the Warden's hand. Rook writes in the
   margin. Marrow comes up with a lamp housing and a ship under the harbour
   ([[The Marked]]).
9. ✔ **Chapter Nine** — the Brine Mother's agent at the foot of the tower, reading
   the contract off a tarred rope. The loophole: any lit lantern settles it, and a
   keeper with no wick left counts as a lantern. The clock: a refusal spoken at the
   Fall before the ninth night of the ninth month transfers the debt to whoever
   refuses. Corbin Sallow, the Understudy, arrives paid twice by two men who want
   opposite things, and meets Marrow ([[The Understudy]]).
10. ✔ **Chapter Ten** — the watch: Teo and Mira, two hours, nothing in particular.
    The best night of the book. Rook sings, badly, from the hold. Sallow does not
    sleep and will not say why. A hatch cover comes loose with Pell under it and
    Mira holds it from thirty feet away, blind, for ninety seconds — **rung two**
    ([[The Ladder]]) — and pays with her grandmother's voice. First use of the
    litany ([[The Coat and the Fire]]).
11. ✔ **Chapter Eleven** — they turn back for the Fold and find a Crown cutter
    already there. **Ossian Vey** first appearance: the licence, the form, the
    courteous refusal of the refusal ([[The Assessor]]). Alys's flare shows the Ash
    twenty days out. She dies at the turn of the tide and pours the last of herself
    into Mira's ring as a **coal** — light with no wick, which Mira cannot use yet
    ([[The Ladder]]). Vey leaves knowing Teo's sight and Sallow's hiring, neither of
    which was in any file.
12. ✔ **Chapter Twelve** — the *Vigil* finds them; cornered in the Gull's Teeth;
    boarding from both sides. Mira spends Alys's coal on **the toll**, a Holding
    shaped as sound, sung by the whole crew off Rook's terrible hymn
    ([[The Toll]]). The boats stop, the Hollowed oarsmen turn and try to remember a
    word, and Vey hears his own voice from the year the Fifth went dark; his ring
    cracks and he breaks off for one hour. Cost: the winter she was twelve. Voss
    sends a boat and an invitation.
13. ✔ **Chapter Thirteen** — Mira aboard the *Vigil* under truce, with Isolde and
    Rook. Beeswax, white gloves, no lantern anywhere. Voss wants the eight dark
    lanterns relit inside eleven days under licence, and she is right about the
    arithmetic. The argument: a licence is a way of holding a light in your hand
    without owning it. Neither of them wins. What she actually wants is a keeper who
    can light a cold lantern quickly — because she is trying to get her brother back,
    and because arithmetic has stopped working. Vey hands Mira a receipt for the coal,
    logged as *unassessed*.

14. ✔ **Chapter Fourteen** — the squall takes the mainsail; Rook brings Pell down off
    the mast and pays for it with his shoulder. That night in the hold he says a new
    vow with Mira for a witness: *I will go where it is needed. Not where I'm sent.*
    Done badly, in front of the crew, and nobody claps. The *Vigil* will beat them to
    the Kindling by sea, so the only way left is the Drowned Road — which needs a
    lens. Ends on the heading for Candlewick.

### Part Three

15. ✔ **Chapter Fifteen** — Candlewick: the tavern that was the Fourth, the great lens
    hung over the bar with candles in it, and Orren Beck — the keep's last keeper, who
    held his six-year-old son up out of the harbour for a count of forty and paid for
    it with every memory of the boy. He wants proof the light matters. Mira lights the
    lens, the drowned quay comes up out of the water, and the whole town stands in it.
    Cost: **the smell of her own kitchen** (rung three's smell). She writes Jory Beck's
    name on a page and Beck pastes it above the bottle shelf and reads it every
    morning.
16. ✔ **Chapter Sixteen** — a lens with no tower will not point, so they lace a canvas
    chimney out of water-butt hoops and the drowned road comes up out of the sea with
    **thirteen** ships sitting on it. The lens tips; Mira takes it and pays nothing she
    can name; Teo puts his hands in the light and holds it **without a ring**, which is
    the third column of the Glass Roll and the reason the Crown keeps a list of the
    marked. Talking keeps him holding. He tells true things, badly. Then he says the
    small kind lie — *I've done this before* — and the lane goes out, the lens slams,
    and one ring of glass shears off. Cost: **Teo's sight of the revealed world**. He
    promises he can still see it. He counts twelve ships; there are thirteen. The fight
    that follows is really a conversation, and both of them confess to lying.
17. ✔ **Chapter Seventeen** — sixty miles of drowned road in the *Tongue*, an open
    boat with the lens on a crutch, travelled twenty feet at a time for two nights.
    The chipped lens throws the road twice: the **copy repeats itself** and has no
    damage in it, because the light shows the road as it *was*. Mira's rule becomes
    *you do not steer by the road, you steer by the damage.* Teo has been blind since
    Candlewick and calling the way by the *tock* a wall makes in shallow water, and
    admits it in the drowned chapel at Coldharbour. Cost: **the smell of a lamp**.
    They come out at Barrowfoot at dawn to find the *Vigil* already anchored and
    waiting, and Mira tells Teo it is fog — her first fluent lie, and it lands.

18. ✔ **Chapter Eighteen** — taken up at Barrowfoot: the lens impounded as evidence,
    Vey's pen, Rook's blade given up hilt-first, forty empty brass brackets and **one
    lit window aft**. Voss lays out a licence and a warrant on the same table — Teo
    safe as a rating, or Teo assessed as *lens-capable* — and asks one thing for free:
    look at her brother. **Konrad Voss**, 51, keeper of the Second when it went down,
    plays chess by being asked *which one?*, wins every game, and cannot ask a question
    — *he will answer anything, he cannot begin*. The window: one light on the flagship,
    nine miles out on the edge of the Ash, and she knows it, and cannot put it out.
    Vey's duplicate Roll: Teo's name, *recovered*, in a hand its own clerk has never met.
    At dusk the lens shows the town full of holes shaped like people — sixty men went up
    that hill — and the holes turn to look at the light. Mira does not sign, and will
    light the Seventh anyway. Ends on the one window still lit.
19. ✔ **Chapter Nineteen** — the Hollow Bell rings in still air, **once every six
    seconds, like a clock in a house with no clock**. Private Tibb, in the cable tier,
    says it was already ringing when the sixty went up the shingle and the captain said
    it was the wind. Voss orders the lens lit and the marines along the chains, because
    *I would rather they came to me* — and the drowned road comes up with **fifty-two**
    figures walking it in single file, holes in the gold, a woman with a basket at the
    front and a child at the back. They do not fight and they do not run: they climb the
    chains and go to work. Rook draws and the blade stays dark — *I have never been
    unable to mean it before* — Pell is handed a rope and takes two turns before he
    understands, and Sergeant Dace puts a hand on a man's chest and is still standing
    there an hour later. The bell counts to **fifty-six** and stops, having saved a place
    for the four already aboard. Mira holds the cockpit hatch for a count of a hundred and
    eighty while they wait on the other side of it, and pays with **the Circlet rhyme, the
    first three years of school, and the name of the family next door**. Then Sallow's
    third clause comes out at his desk, mid-crisis, in a neat hand: six salt a week, a box
    on the quay at Saltmere, and the words *nobody asked*. Marrow answers him with one
    word. Konrad Voss stands up for the first time in eleven years — *what are you waiting
    for?* / *the bell* — and Voss gives Mira the boat, the lens and a receipt, and takes
    her ship out to be lost with fifty-six of the Ash's people aboard her and not one man
    of her own.
20. ✔ **Chapter Twenty** — the road south. The *Vigil* comes down the coast with her
    yards square, three days after the Admiral put her on the north rocks — *she was on
    the north rocks at five* — and Mira turns the boat around: nobody has ever chosen the
    grandmother. Three nights south past holes where the Ash has eaten the road on its
    way home, and the copy shows Carrow before the Fall, whole and lit, with a woman on
    the sea-wall doing the round she was doing four hundred years ago. The real island is
    dark and alive and doesn't ask. **Ede Calloway, 71**, walks the wall with matches in
    every pocket and a dead ring on her hand, holding a **ring** round the whole island at
    the waterline — no colour, no glow, nothing to study, so the Ash finds a door it cannot
    see the end of and goes home again. Cost: **a season a year, eleven seasons gone**. She
    asks the date and writes it down; she writes fifty names every morning so she will know
    who she has lost, and Mira's is always at the top. The photograph: *is this you?* — *it
    was.* The book: *I have put it out*, and the woman with the bad hand in the kitchen, and
    the child. Mira says the counter-vow to her own grandmother. Rook asks what he can do
    that is not a writ, and gets a bucket. Ends with one window lit nine miles out, four
    boats on the booms, and *then I have been holding the wrong door.*
21. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-One** — first light, the ring comes down, and the sea inside it gets
    its head up. Rook keeps the bucket vow — *a hundred and four steps, six of them are
    wide.* The lamp room is **ready**, not clean: brass polished, wick trimmed, five hundred
    and seventy entries reading *dark, wick trimmed, nothing to report*, and one that
    doesn't. The names read aloud: a hundred and forty of them, said back in a flat careful
    voice — including **the Gannets**, the family next door, whose name Mira lost at
    Barrowfoot and whose gate she can still see. Then the last page: **Tess Calloway**, four
    hundred times, crossed out and written again. Her mother. *Say it. Again.* Rook wants to
    light the tower and hold the Ash at the wall; **Sallow** says the thing nobody wants
    heard — it is not here for the wall, it is here for the girl, and it has been following
    the light since Sixthmonth. So the light leaves: Mira takes it north as bait and the
    siege goes with her. At the slip she says *grandmother*, twice; Ede tears the page out of
    the book and gives it to her, and gives her dead ring to Rook. At dusk she holds a lane
    for a count of four hundred to bring a three-decker's anchor up — and the bill is the
    name she was given that morning. **End of Part Three.**

### Part Four

22. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Two** — the road goes under the Kindling and up a stair: three hundred
    steps, an iron door with nine slots and eight pins, and a woman waiting behind it with a
    **green band on her wrist that shimmers and turns towards Mira's ring**. The fortress at
    dawn, Marshal Hale — *thank God* — and eight Crown marines and a clerk with their names on
    a list. The catechism at the change of the watch: a hundred knights, and Rook's mouth
    saying *I will guide no ship that the Throne has not counted* while his hands hang at his
    sides. **Warden Ansel Halloran** gives her the arithmetic in the first person, signed the
    schedule eleven years ago, keeps the Ninth's deferral in his own hand, and asks the one
    thing that would work: *stop being seen.* Downstairs, **Odo Pike** and the eleven guides
    keep the order's struck-out first purpose — a ring holds, a **band** goes; a band can be
    shared, remembered, put down, lent to the dying; it costs **hours** out of the middle of a
    life. In the case on the wall, the **caramel light** — amber, asleep, one in four hundred
    years, Brinehollow, four hundred people who could not say their children's names. Rook
    learns what the reform took. Mira takes a band, and the road opens.
23. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Three** — the receipt in the drawer: four lines, eight lights withdrawn
    *for a term of eleven years and a day*, in consideration whereof the Crown undertakes to
    keep the coast by other means. Signed at Saltmere; witnessed by the Warden in the largest
    hand on the page. **Then the machine:** the Warden takes her down the chimney stair to the
    forge — a wheel forty feet across, no fire anywhere, and the **mother** in a cradle of iron,
    the grey stone the size of a cart that is all that is left of the sun inside the Circlet.
    Nine sockets round it, all nine empty: eight are in the towers and the ninth is in the brass
    cup of a chipped lens in a canvas bag on the *Mercy Bell*. An Ember is a fist of the mother quenched in **a keeper's season**, which is why
    there have been nine in four hundred years, and why the Crown's first act in the towers was
    to forbid the cutting — *you can count a ship; you cannot count a girl at a trough.* Then
    the quieter thing, from Brother Ostrand's ledger with both hands flat on the page:
    **Attendance at the dark keeps, eight, per quarter, 240 salt** — eleven years of the Crown
    paying the order to stand in dark rooms and be seen to, and that stipend is the collateral
    behind every salt-letter on the coast. Rook: *I have eaten at that table for twelve years.*
    The term ends on **the ninth night of the ninth month** — the same night
    the Brine Mother collects. The fleet's sailings for eleven years make four ships: a
    survey, a transport of auditors, and two wrecks. The *Mercy Bell* comes in past the Ash
    and the Ash lets her by, *because she is not a light.* Eleven of the crew are the drowned:
    they take the guides' bands, and the band shows them the **deep road** past the shelf where
    living guides go blind — the price takes nothing from them, and Odo Pike says a band that
    takes nothing is owed somewhere. Rill has to hold Marrow's hand at the two-hundredth step.
    Marrow sings the tide-bell of the Sill and cannot say how he knows it. **Sallow** refuses,
    and takes it when Marrow says *I've got the way and no words in my head — you've got the
    words.* Isolde, watching: *I'm jealous.*
24. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Four** — Thursday, on the tide. Sallow gets the *Tongue* out at first
    light with the chipped lens and the Ember in the brass cup — *a boat that goes out at dawn is
    a fishing boat* — and Teo is told to come back at dusk and not before, and to go west and not
    come back at all if there is a fleet. There is a fleet: nine sail on the turn, the ***Surety***
    first. **Ilse Voss** lands with a case holding **three Embers — the Second, the Seventh and
    the Eighth** (the Eighth has soot on it; Alys Wren is a line in a manifest) and a writ that
    names the **forge, the mother, the trough**, the Ninth's Ember, and Mira's examination under
    the Glass Roll. She offers a chair, a salary and a sergeant at the door. She wrote the
    stipend schedule herself and has signed every quarter since, and says so before Mira can say
    it at her: *I have never once been able to work out whether I am the woman who saved the
    coast or the woman who sold it.* Mira offers the third thing — *take me instead of the coast
    for nine days.* The boats are searched at four and the frame is empty. Mira goes aboard the
    *Surety* at six, politely, with the notebook. Voss on the quarterdeck: the chessboard, Konrad,
    one question — *where is my Ember?* — and the window goes gold. Teo four hundred yards off the
    rocks with the lane lit, the road up, and **eighty holes** standing where the fleet is. The
    parley: *Why are you here?* — **"Because I love her."** (a fact, stated to a third party, in
    the middle of something worse). Terms struck boat to boat in the dark: forge sealed for nine
    days, guides and drowned **off the Roll** for nine, the Ninth's Ember into the strongroom as
    Crown property — logged, numbered, ***unassessed*** — and Mira on a warrant of sufferance that
    dies with the term. Price: Teo holds a lane with no ring and pays with **the harbour** — he
    cannot find the way in any more — and Mira counts him in from four hundred yards with the wall
    at forty feet. The Ash's window comes in from nine miles to five. Isolde on the slip: *what's
    it worth, then?*
25. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Five** — the exchange: the Crown does not sell, it **exchanges**, and Vey
    values the Ninth's Ember at one lugger, ninety feet. Isolde signs at twelve minutes past nine
    and finds out her crew goes with the ship, because a ship's people are appurtenant and the
    Crown does not buy wrecks. Then the wind comes — the first in five days — and with it a black
    ship with **three red lights** at the masthead, and **the Ash runs from it**: the window goes
    dark, comes back a mile south, and is gone. *The Scarlet Assize.* The Warden names the first
    heresy: an Ember quenched in **a season taken**, so the air goes cold and a man beside a red
    blade loses the hour, then the year, then his name. **Anselm Cray** reads a list of the
    coast's failures in the yard and states the doctrine — something behind the Ash eats coasts
    that have never been hard; they are *the tempering before it* — and demands the mother and the
    nine in eight nights. The fight at the gate: forty marines and eleven pirates holding a line
    for six minutes, Lieutenant **Airey emptied in the doorway** and left alive to teach the
    lesson, four drowned men emptied of the deep road, a guide's band cracked. Cray cuts the drum
    of the nine-slot door through, leaves a **red blade burning in the stair**, and spares the
    Warden to carry the message. And he leaves his novice: **Toby Larch**, sixteen, on a bollard
    with a **teal blade** that has never lit — and lights when Mira's ring comes near it.
26. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Six** — the stair, Saturday. The red blade takes the morning off Rill in
    twenty steps and shuts the deep road for fourteen hours. **Only given light can put out taken
    light**, and the only given blade in the fortress belongs to a knight who cannot be ordered to
    use it: *a man cannot mean an order.* Halloran refuses three times and forbids nothing, which is
    the only thing he can do that is not an order. In the archive, Mira finds the one leaf in four
    hundred years: **Ser Aldous Mere at Ostrey**, who did it once, whose blade went dark and never
    lit again, who could not say the vows afterwards and lived nineteen years as a lay brother. In
    the margin, in his own hand: *it does not fight. It opens.* At dusk the three questions are put
    to Rook on the stair and he cannot answer the first two and says *I will* to the third, and the
    blade comes up gold and goes into the red — and the red lets go of everything it holds, through
    him: a woman at a window, barrels counted on a quay, a boy promising the tide, Lieutenant Airey
    with the Admiral's compliments, a log read aloud, Harrow, and Brinehollow with Rook's own voice
    at nineteen saying *hold the door*. Four minutes. The red goes out, the sword goes out and stays
    out, and afterwards he cannot say the vows — and Halloran says *good*, confirms him a second
    time, takes the surcoat and leaves him the dead iron. Toby's teal blade comes up by the fire and
    stays up, because he listened. Bar the door with oak and iron and a bell: six nights left.
27. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Seven** — Sunday: the council in the guides' room. The Ash is not coming:
    it is going **south-west to the Fall**, and every un-ship and every Hollowed on the coast with
    it. Hale puts on a **yellow watch band** and sees nine hundred holes walking south and, at the
    end of the road, the place where the road is not there. Vey: *I have never once asked what they
    were for.* Isolde: it is not a war, it is a creditor getting her books in order. The **four
    colours** on the old roll — green for the road, yellow for the watch (paid by a parish), orange
    for the caramel, white scratched out — and the **guides' vow**, five lines, the fifth struck out
    by the Crown: *we will guide any ship that asks.* Then the plan, which is Mira's: **nine lights
    at the foot of the Fall on the ninth night, all lit, all seen**, nine keepers holding one ring
    with no ends in it. The price nobody says out loud until Halloran does: the Fall is not Ede's
    four miles — nine keepers means **nine seasons out of nine people in one night**, and five of the
    nine have never held anything bigger than a wick, and a ring with a hole in it is a line and a
    line has ends. It needs the five Embers out of the Crown's five lit towers — douse the only
    lights on the coast — and a warrant Voss will sign with six days of authority left. Vey: five
    stones are not five keepers. Monday: the captains' council aboard the *Surety*, the motion put
    and **lost** — nine for, none against, fourteen abstaining — Kettle's *that's not a plan, that's
    a wake with a schedule*, and Voss saying she is going anyway. Fourteen free crews come in because Isolde went round four
    harbours in a borrowed cutter; twenty-three sail in the roads. Voss tells the crews the vote
    failed and offers a free passage; Mira says the long vow to them and gets *answer for* right, and
    tells them it does not say win. Kettle takes his hat off and puts his hand up, and twenty-two of
    twenty-three follow — *the Ivy is a sensible ship* — and Voss orders one hooded lamp at every
    masthead. Then the guides take their sleeves up on the quay, the green goes along the
    line, and two hundred years of walking in the dark end in front of twenty-three sail. Teo counts
    the harbour he cannot see; Toby gets his first lesson in what is under the floor.

### Part Five

28. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Eight** — Tuesday: the cutters come back with four keepers and one letter — the
    Sixth will not douse the only light on the north coast, and his refusal is the most honest thing
    in the file. Four keepers practise on the water; Ede comes north from Carrow (**a ring is a thing
    with no ends and a shore is a thing made of ends** — so hold a horseshoe, both ends on rock, nine
    miles of lip); Teo holds a hundred and forty and pays with **the sound of the sea**; Vey holds
    because he has written the whole coast down. Wednesday: Halloran stays with the ninth pin and a
    doorway (*bring the keeper home*). Thursday: the guides take twenty-two sail through the dark
    water all night, bands lit, calling the road ship to ship — the fifth line of their vow, at last,
    for ships. Friday, the ninth: **the Bowl at the foot of the Fall**. Nine un-ships in a ring, nine
    hundred Hollowed standing on the shingle, the *Vigil* with her window, the noise in the teeth.
    **Voss finds Konrad in the third row** and he says her name when she asks it. **The Receiver**
    walks out of the water with the Mother's book: eleven years of light at nine lanterns, payable in
    light **or in what light has taken** — nine seasons out of nine keepers at the lip by the turn of
    the tide, or a collection in people, one at a time. Vey learns the *Mercy Bell* and her company
    are the collateral, because a ship's people are appurtenant and he wrote that clause himself.
    **At four the collection begins**: the Hollowed walk over the lip one at a time and nobody can
    stop it, and the Ash comes ashore at last — a shape a mile long standing up out of the mist with
    one foot on the shingle. Nine gone over. Three hours to the turn. Nine lights, eight Embers, one
    anchor empty.
29. ✔ **Chapter Twenty-Nine** — the turn of the tide at the Fall. Seven boats strung along nine miles
    of lip, the guides standing in the shallows behind each keeper calling the shelf, and a boy with a
    teal blade in the empty north housing. At six the collection quickens and **Sallow counts it out
    loud** — *somebody has to count, it is the whole of my trade* — to eleven at four minutes to
    seven, and one of the eleven is **Konrad Voss**, who comes down in his good coat, looks at his
    sister with the mild interest of a man acknowledging a stranger, and walks on; the Admiral puts
    the hour in the book and goes to stand at the north rock. At seven the ring goes up and the sea
    goes flat and the road comes up **black and dry** and goes over the edge of the Fall and keeps
    going, and the collection stops dead against the inside of the wall. **Kettle rows** — forty
    boats inside the ring, bow to stern, one boat at a time all night — and Marrow sings the tide-bell
    of the Sill and eleven boats sing it wrong with him, and the mist answers. The thing comes along
    the ring looking for an end the way a man runs his hand along a wall in a dark house; the Hollowed
    press against it like rain on a window; Pell sees Mrs Arden from Carrow in a blue coat and is told
    no. **The Receiver counts**: Mira (**the vow** — the words stay on page nine and the joining of
    them is gone, and she finds it out with nine miles of wall in her hands), Ede, Meridew (her
    daughter's name), the
    tavern-keeper, Vey (twice — and he can write and cannot read), Teo (a dock), Isolde (the name of
    her ship). The tavern-keeper's body goes at twenty past seven and Vey takes the hole with one ring
    and both arms. Eight seasons and one short: the ninth coin is **a light that has been given** —
    Toby Larch gives the teal blade, seventeen volunteered seasons, *tell him I learned what a kept
    coast is*, and it goes up instead of out and leaves a small light on the rock. **The account is
    closed.** Twenty went over the lip in all and the Fall goes quiet, and the grey shape is gone off
    the shingle, and the eight hundred and eighty who are left stop walking and stand in the rain.
    The Receiver: *the Mother will trouble your children's — that is not a threat, it is a schedule.*
    Rook says the fifth line over them and means to walk in front, and Mira says *we will* and nothing
    after it, and Ede tells her the thing nobody tells you: it is not the words that keep a light, it
    is the saying. Isolde, on the shingle, at last
    tells Mira what she saw in her eleven minutes: **a door standing open** at the bottom of the Reach
    with a day behind it and a man in a good coat in the doorway saying *you're early* and *tell them
    the road goes on.*
30. ✔ **Chapter Thirty** — dawn on the tenth: **no authority on the Reach** (the term died at
    midnight; Vey has no idea what to do with his hands), the **Assize** stands off the bay at first
    light and dips its three lights and goes — *there is nothing in his doctrine for a coast that
    paid*. **Voss says the true thing in front of a witness** on the shingle, and Ede hears it and
    gives her a wick-knife and a job (the Second's room at Brinehollow, full of salt to the height of
    a man); she gives the fleet away at noon and the crews take their hats off and she tells them to
    put them back on; she asks Mira for the one entry that gets carried, and it is Konrad's. **The
    walk**: 880 people who cannot be spoken to will not follow a lamp on a pole, so they follow a
    window — a launch with a dodger rigged behind the bow lamp, north at the pace of a walking man;
    guides go into each house first, light the kitchen, stand in the doorway and **say the name**. A
    hollowed lullaby on the second night that nobody's mouth is making; Pell keeps watch. **Candlewick**:
    Orren Beck comes home to a pub he does not know, and Jory reads the page above the bottle shelf
    aloud, and the Fourth is kept by a son taught by a father who cannot remember him. **Isolde gets
    her ship back** when the terms lapse and gets her name back off a page. **The pair**, three days
    short of Carrow: the counter-vow paid — *if I forget you / then I'll introduce myself again* — and
    Ede's verse said badly, both stanzas, with the third line argued over. **Carrow**: Ede puts her
    ring down on the sea-wall; Mira climbs 112 steps, cannot say the vow, says seven words out of the
    middle of it — *I am here, and I can see* — and the lamp takes it and **charges her nothing**,
    which frightens her more than any price. Then a season of lights: Vey at Saltmere dictating an
    entry he cannot read; Meridew at the Sill answering for what she can see; Jory's forty candles;
    Voss cleaning a room she may not light; the Sixth's four-line letter; the Keeper of the Fall.
    Hale brings four lines from the Throne (an apology is not among them) and a commission she intends
    to write herself. The last page: the short form, the cost, the names, and her own name in her own
    hand — *I lit it.* The sea did not mind. The light did.

## Register check

Three voices, and the book is only itself when all three are in play — see
[[Lines and Flair]].

- **Rogue (Isolde):** Chapters 4, 6, 9, 13, 25. Stops dead at Chapter 27; in Part
  Five she stops performing, and the crew notices before Mira does.
- **Oath (Rook, the Warden):** 5, 14, 22, 23, 26, 27. Two words or four, never a
  speech, except the once at the Kindling.
- **Land (narration):** chapter openings, the last paragraph of each part, and the
  last line of the book. Short, flat sentences. The sea does not mind.

Two flair lines a chapter. If a chapter needs a third, it is carrying a joke that
belongs to somebody else's scene.

## Open questions

- Who put out the Ninth eleven years ago — the Crown, or Ede, to keep Mira alive?
  (Answer: both. Voss stood in Ede's kitchen in the snow, with the scarred hand and a
  receipt, and told her the light was a door and the dark was a harbour and that if the
  door was shut the child would live. Ede put it out of her own will — her book says so,
  in her own hand, in the syntax of a woman being careful — and the Ash took the night
  itself with the first season. Answered in Chapter Twenty, from the page.)
- What has Ede been doing since? (Standing on the sea-wall at Carrow every night, holding
  a ring round the island with no light in it. It worked because it could not be studied.
  The Ash now has a hull, so it does not need the road, and the ring is a door in a house
  that has other doors.)
- Why does Teo see un-ships without a ring? (He was born on the dark water, on a
  ship that was not there.)
- What did Isolde see in her eleven minutes dead? (The other side of the Circlet: a
  door standing open under the third bell with a day behind it, and a man in a good
  coat in the doorway. She tells Mira in Chapter Twenty-Nine, on the shingle, after
  she has already paid for it with the name of her ship. She keeps the vision and
  loses the name — the light takes the cheap thing and leaves the costly one, which
  is the most frightening thing in the book.)
- Did Alder Vane actually go over the Fall? (Yes. Isolde's eleven minutes are the
  proof: the man in the doorway is a hanged captain who says *you're early* and
  *tell them the road goes on.* Keep the Crown's denial credible: it is easier to
  hang a liar than to believe him.)
- Which Vane is Teo? (Deliberately unanswered. The name is common; the docking is
  not. Answer in book two or not at all.)
- Is the Brine Mother the Ash's opposite? (No. She is a creditor. That is the
  joke and the horror.)
