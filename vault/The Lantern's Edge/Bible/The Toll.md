# The Toll

*The one thing Mira does in [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]] that nobody taught
her: a Holding shaped as sound instead of a shape.*

## What it is

A Holding is will given a shape. But a shape needs to be **seen**, and sight is the
first thing this coast takes away — the blind, the Hollowed, the men below decks, the
ships beyond the light.

So: give the will a **sound** instead. Not a shout, not a spell. A note, held the way
a rope is held, and pushed out across the water like the stroke of a bell.

The keeper does not sing *at* anyone. She sings the thing true, and the light carries
it, and everyone the wave passes through hears it in their own head, in their own
voice, at their own volume.

## The three laws

1. **Sound needs agreement.** One voice is a shout. Three voices meaning the same
   thing is a law. The toll's reach is measured in people who mean it, not in miles —
   which is why it works at the Gull's Teeth and would not work in an empty sea.
2. **Sound does not need sight.** It reaches the blind, the Hollowed, the drowned,
   the men rowing with their backs turned, and things under the water. It also
   reaches what cannot be looked at directly, which is the only reason anyone
   survives Chapter Twelve.
3. **It strips.** Everyone the wave passes through hears the truest thing they know,
   once, in their own voice. Not a judgement and not a vision. A sentence they have
   been refusing to say to themselves, in the accent they were born with.

There is no cleansing in it. The coast's word for it is *the washing*, and the
keepers' word is *the toll*, and neither word means forgiveness. It means you heard
it.

## What it does to different listeners

- **The crew** — nothing dramatic. They hear the thing they already knew and have
  been carrying: Isolde hears the ship's ledger; Pell hears that he is not a coward;
  Rook hears the third question nobody asks any more.
- **The Hollowed** — the terrible one. They stop, and they *turn towards it*, and
  some of them begin to make a sound back. Not words. The shape of words. It lasts as
  long as the toll and then they are empty again, and the emptiness is worse for
  having been interrupted.
- **The Crown's sailors** — ordinary frightened men hear ordinary frightened things
  and stop rowing. That is the tactical effect and it is not to be underestimated.
- **The Ash** — it does not stop. It *hesitates*, the way a wave hesitates at a wall,
  and then goes round. The toll buys minutes, never miles.
- **Vey** — he hears his own voice at nineteen, holding the wick, saying nothing. He
  has been filing that sentence under procedure for eleven years and the toll reads
  it back to him in his own accent.

## The cost

A toll is not a rung. It cannot be practised into and it cannot be repeated:

- **It needs a coal** — somebody else's lit will, given freely (see [[The Ladder]]).
  Mira has exactly one, from Alys Wren, and it goes out in Chapter Twelve.
- **The keeper pays a season.** Not a smell, not a name: a whole season of one year,
  the weather and the food and the people in it. She keeps the fact and loses the
  time.
- **The singers pay small.** A song, a name, the memory of a tune. Nobody notices
  until the next time somebody starts it and only half the crew can join in.
- **It is used once.** Once in this book, and once ever, by this keeper. If it could
  be repeated it would be a weapon, and the book would be about a weapon.

## Why it beats a man like Vey

He cannot be beaten by force — he never fights. He cannot be beaten by argument — he
wins arguments for a living. He cannot be beaten by law — he *is* the law's copy.

He can only be beaten by being **heard**. The toll does not take his licence, his
ledger or his clerk. It gives him one sentence in his own voice, and he cannot
un-hear it, and for one hour of his life he is a man who was in the room and did
nothing, rather than an official following procedure.

Then he goes back to work. That is the point: being heard is not the same as being
changed, and the book should not pretend otherwise.

## Rules of use

- **Never describe it as music.** It is a note, held, like a rope held. Nobody says
  "melody" or "harmony" in the prose.
- **The prose goes short.** At the moment the toll leaves her, the sentences should
  be four words long.
- **The reader must hear it too.** Put the reader inside one listener's head — a
  Crown sailor, a Hollowed child — for two sentences, no more.
- **No glowing.** The light goes *out* of her hand and into the air. She should look
  like a woman standing in the dark with nothing, which is what she is.
