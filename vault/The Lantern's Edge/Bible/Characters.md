# Characters

*Everyone in [[The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible]] wants something they cannot
say out loud. That is the whole trick of the book.*

## Mira Calloway — 18, keeper of the Ninth

**Wants:** to matter. To be the person the notebook was written for.
**Wound:** her mother, **Tess Calloway**, went into the water the night the lights went
out, when Mira was seven. Nobody in Carrow will talk about it in a way that adds up,
and the one person who has the date written down cannot remember her face either. See
Ede, below; the name is lost in Chapter Twenty-One.
**Lie she believes:** *if I am useful, I will be loved.*
**Arc:** from lighting a lamp to prove herself, to choosing what stays lit — and
to letting one person love her without earning it.
**Voice:** counts things. Says the plain true thing at the worst possible moment.
**Tag:** her grandmother's coat, too big at the shoulders; chapped hands; a pencil
behind her ear; the notebook, page nineteen.
**Tell:** when she is frightened she becomes extremely polite.
**The ladder:** rung one in the tower, rung two in the middle watch. She experiences
growth as arithmetic and fear, never as power, and the people who love her get
frightened before she does. See [[The Ladder]].
**The litany:** *I am Mira Calloway, keeper of the Ninth, and I am not afraid* — said
only when it is nearly a lie, and eroding by Part Five. See [[The Coat and the Fire]].

## Teodor "Teo" Vane — 19, navigator of the *Mercy Bell*

**Wants:** to be free of the Bell and the debt that keeps him aboard.
**Wound:** left on a dock as a child; the sea took him in and never gave him a
name that was his.
**Lie he believes:** *if I never stay, I can never be left.*
**Arc:** from running to staying — first for the ship, then for Mira, then for a
coast he has spent his life robbing.
**Voice:** jokes first, truth second, and the jokes are always more specific than
the truth. Lies with exact details. Cannot lie in lantern light, which is a
problem he does not know about yet.
**Tag:** too many rings and none of them glass; a coat stolen from a man who no
longer needs it; a scar on his jaw he claims is from a duel and which is from a
door.
**The name:** Vane is the commonest salt-name on the Reach, given to a hundred boys
the year the captain was hanged. Teo has an answer ready for the question and has
never once had to use it, which he thinks about more than he would like.
**The rings:** seven, and exactly one of them was a gift — Sallow's, the flawed one
on the little finger. The other six came two for a quarter-mark off a stall in
Saltmere, and he tells people they were given to him, with names and occasions,
because a bought ring looks like a lonely thing. Small lie, told well.
**The lens (Ch. 16):** a marked man can hold a light with no ring at all, which is
the third column of the Glass Roll — *lens-capable* — and the order files it as a
mercy. No ring means nothing between him and the price. He holds a lane for a
count and pays with his sight of the revealed world: still water, no road, no
ships. He says he can still see it, and can't even keep the count straight.
**The lie in the light (Ch. 16):** the big true thing — *I'm not going to leave* —
holds. It is the small kind sentence after it, *it's all right, I've done this
before*, that the lane refuses. He lies to make people feel better, and it is the
only lie he cannot stop telling.
**The wall (Ch. 17):** blind to everything the light shows, he navigates sixty miles
of drowned road by the *tock* a stone wall makes in three feet of water, and by
arithmetic, and by the confidence in his own voice. Two days of not saying it, in
front of the crew, because *if I'd said I can't see it, the last thing anybody had
would have gone out of the room*. He tells Mira in a drowned chapel at dawn, and then
teaches her to hear it with her hand on the gunwale — which is the nearest thing to a
love scene the book has before Chapter Twenty-Four.
**The Roll (Ch. 18):** his entry reads *Vane, Teodor. Parish: none. Keeper: none.
Class: glass-eyed, unassessed. Disposition: recovered* — taken off the lugger *Anselm*,
found afloat nine days after she went down with all hands, one child aboard. It is the
word the Crown uses for cargo and for bodies, and he has been on a page since he was
six without ever being assessed. He does not know how to feel about being written down,
and settles on *I'd rather be on your page than theirs*. Mira writes him into the back
of the notebook with herself as keeper and *Disposition: staying*, and he points out
that this is not how a roll works.
**The bell (Ch. 19):** blind to what the light shows, he hears the Hollow Bell before
anyone else and hears it *twice* — once in the tower and once coming up through the
hull — and says so flatly, and nobody asks how he knows any more. He is the one who
says a bell is not a bell on a ship.
**Tell:** he touches the rail before he says anything he means.

## Captain Isolde Sable — 46, the *Mercy Bell*

**Wants:** to clear her debt to the Brine Mother before the Mother collects in a
currency she would rather keep.
**Wound:** she was dead for eleven minutes at the bottom of the Reach, and she
remembers all of it, and she has never told anyone what she saw.
**Lie she believes:** *everything is a transaction; if I keep the ledger, nobody
can take anything from me.*
**Arc:** from ledger to family. She starts the book planning to sell a lantern
and ends it planning to die for a girl who is not hers.
**Voice:** nautical epithets, appalling jokes at funerals, the only person aboard
who is never seasick and never surprised.
**Tag:** a hat she does not take off indoors; a very good knife she uses as a
fork.
**The band (Ch. 23):** she brings eleven drowned men home to a cellar where a stranger hands
them roads, and watches her crew go down on their knees to look at where they drowned, and
says the only true thing about herself in the book: *I'm jealous.* Forty years of knowing
where the ship was going and never once where she was.
**The debt (Ch. 28):** the Receiver tells her the *Mercy Bell* — hull and company — was raised on
terms and is entered in the Mother's book as Crown property, because she sold it and a ship's people
are appurtenant. Her answer is not a legal one: *there is nothing down there that is going to be
impressed by a ring. Put me in the water and I will hold the rope.*
**The sale (Ch. 25):** she sells the *Mercy Bell* to the Crown for the Ninth's Ember — 9.12 in the
morning, the hour written in because she asked for it — and discovers in the same breath that a
ship's people are appurtenant and that she has sold her crew with her ship. She tells them they
are navy, and that they will stand where they are told, and that when it matters they will do the
ship, the coast and each other, in that order. Then she stands on the quay all afternoon watching
a Crown pennant go up her mainmast, because a woman who has just traded forty years for a stone
does not get to go indoors.
**Tell:** she lies by telling the truth in the wrong order.

## Ser Cadan Rook — 26, Emberwatch

**Wants:** to be worthy of a vow he made at fourteen to a god he now suspects was
a machine.
**Wound:** he obeyed an order that drowned Brinehollow, and was decorated for it.
Wren's Fold is still ahead of him and he knows it.
**Lie he believes:** *obedience and goodness are the same thing.*
**Arc:** obedience → conscience → a choice that costs him the order, his cloak and
almost the arm. In Chapter Fourteen he says the Long Night's three questions again,
to a keeper instead of an order: *I will go where it is needed. Not where I'm sent.*
He says it badly, in a hold, and Mira writes it down because she is the only witness
alive and that is now a thing she can be.
**Voice:** formal, literal, quietly funny by accident. Says "I will" instead of
"yes".
**Tag:** a two-handed ember-blade that lights when he means it — which means he
cannot lie to himself in front of witnesses. Ash-white surcoat folded in three,
iron thumb-ring, one bag with a comb, a shirt, a whetstone, a spoon and a book, and
no money at all.
**The rule:** a knight of the watch keeps no light of his own. He sits in the dark
for an hour a day because of it, which is where the dark starts talking to him.

**The stair (Ch. 26):** he reads the order's one leaf — Ser Aldous Mere, Ostrey, a given blade put
out a taken one and never lit again — and says the only thing that matters about it: *he did it
alone. That's the part I can fix.* Then he asks for the stair three times, is refused three times,
and goes anyway, which is the first disobedience of his life. He cannot answer *will you keep what
is not yours* or *will you go where you are sent*, and he answers *will you be seen* with *I will*,
and the blade comes up. Four minutes later the red has let go of nineteen years through him and his
sword is dead iron.
**What he loses:** not a season. The vows. He opens his mouth on the stair and the words are not
true any more, and a knight of the watch cannot say a false thing in front of a witness, so he says
nothing, and Halloran says **good**, and confirms him a second time, and takes the surcoat and
leaves him the iron. He keeps the dead sword because *a man who has spent his certainty on a door
is entitled to keep the iron he did it with.*
**Where he is going:** the vow he said badly in a hold in Ch. 14 — *I will go where it is needed.
Not where I'm sent* — is now the only one he has left, and by Ch. 27 he can say it and mean it.
**Voice after Ch. 26:** shorter. He stops qualifying. He says *I don't know* and it costs him
nothing, which is how the reader knows what changed.
**The Long Night:** three questions at dawn when he was fourteen — *will you keep
what is not yours, will you go where you are sent, will you be seen* — and three
answers of *I will* that he has never been able to spend cheaply. See
[[The Emberwatch]].
**The third column (Ch. 16):** he has carried a copy of the Glass Roll since he was
nineteen and has never read *lens-capable* out loud until Teo stands in the light
holding a lane with no ring. He explains it properly, because that is what he does
with horror: a capable subject may be employed at a lens, no consent, no term. He
says the word *wall* and hears himself say it.
**Tell:** he salutes people he is about to arrest.
**What he believes:** that the order's vows are good and the Crown's clause is a
notary's error, and that if he obeys well enough the difference will stop mattering.
It does not stop mattering, and Chapter Twenty-Two is the bill.
**The words he knows:** the Crown's catechism, by heart, since he was fourteen —
*I will be silent in the light and obedient in the dark.* He has never heard the
old vow until the Kindling, and he recognises the rhythm of it like a song in a
language he does not speak. See [[The Vow and the Last Word]].

## Marrow — the *Mercy Bell*'s bosun, age unknown

**Wants:** nothing he can name. To be on the ship when it matters.
**Wound:** forty years on the bottom of the Reach with a dead crew, and no memory of
any of it — not the crew, not the water, not the man he was on the way down.
**Lie he believes:** none. He is the only person in the book who does not lie to
himself, which is why nobody can talk to him for long.
**Arc:** he goes into the water, and comes back, and the thing he brings up changes
what the others are looking for.
**Voice:** eleven words a day, in a voice like a door. "I'm not shy. I'm quiet."
**Tag:** hands like hooks, a rope over one shoulder, water running off him indoors.
**The band (Ch. 23):** Odo Pike's band, with four hundred miles of road in it, and the thing it
shows him: the road goes *down*. He kneels on the cellar boards and sees where his ship went
down, and he cannot remember one face of the crew that went with him. He takes the band and
walks Odo Pike's roads before his own, and he leads a fourteen-year-old guide past the shelf
by the hand. The band takes nothing from him — *there's no hour in me that anybody's using* —
and Odo Pike says that is the worst thing anybody has said to him in that yard, because
nothing on this coast is free. He comes back up singing the tide-bell of the Sill and cannot
say how he knows it. See [[The Bands]].
**Tell:** he answers the question you did not ask about the water.

## Corbin Sallow — the Understudy, 41 and forty

**Wants:** to be paid, in advance, and to be in the room when it happens.
**Wound:** he came up off the bottom with the rest of the *Mercy Bell*'s crew and
walked away from them in the first week, and has been explaining it ever since.
**Lie he believes:** *if I narrate it, it's a job, and nothing that happens to me is
final.*
**Arc:** from the man who explains the play to the man who is in it. He is funny for
five chapters and not funny at all in the last one.
**Voice:** runs on. Stage cadence, price list, addresses the house. Calls violence
*paperwork* and death *a scandal*.
**Tag:** a red-brown coat too good for the coast, two swords worn low and badly, an
oiled card of rates, a pencil behind the ear — the one thing he has in common with
Mira.
**The third clause (Ch. 19):** six salt a week and a box on the quay at Saltmere — who
is aboard, who is marked, what the light is doing. He writes the night of the Hollow
Bell down while it is happening, accurately, with the ship going quiet around him, and
he does not deny one word of it and does not apologise. His defence is true and not
good: *nobody asked*. Marrow answers him with one word, and it is the first thing in
forty years Sallow has not been able to put a price on.
**The band (Ch. 23):** he refuses it first, and the refusal is the truest thing he ever says:
*the one thing I have never been able to do is see it without narrating it.* Marrow overrules
him — *I've got the way and no words in my head. You've got the words* — and he puts it on
badly and has to be helped with the clasp by a boy of fourteen. It is the first thing in forty
years he has been given that he cannot price.
**Tell:** his hands come down out of the air when he means something.
**Pairing:** Marrow. The two drowned men. One stayed, one left, and neither will say
which was braver. Full note: [[The Understudy]].

## Alys Wren — 34, the Bright of the Eighth

**Wants:** to put it down before she forgets why she is holding it.
**Wound:** she said no to her father, and he went down the stairs and put the family
 to bed, and they came up at the end to ask her to supper.
**Lie she believes:** *if I stop, the eleven years were wasted* — which is Ede's lie
in a younger mouth, and the book wants you to notice.
**Arc:** from endurance to a choice. She is the one person the light has not beaten,
and the price is that she is not entirely a woman any more.
**Voice:** ordinary, tired, wry; counts people out loud to keep them.
**Tag:** a ring gone white all the way through; fingers that are not quite solid at
the tips; a pencil list of names written worse each morning.
**Tell:** she asks you to say the names back to her.

## Sister Ambry — 80, the almshouse at the Sill

**Wants:** that the marked should have somewhere to sleep that is not a cell.
**Wound:** forty years as a keeper, and the ring she gave up rather than use.
**Lie she believes:** *I have earned the right to decide what people are better off
forgetting.*
**Arc:** she is the shelter that has become a cage, and she knows it, and she does
not stop.
**Voice:** kind, precise, and used to being the only authority in the room.
**Tag:** blind, and she looks at a lit window rather than at you.
**Tell:** she tells you what you were doing the last time you were in that room.

## Orren Beck — 55, last keeper of the Fourth

**Wants:** to be worth the thing he kept, and to stop being asked to sell it.
**Wound:** he held his six-year-old son up out of Candlewick harbour for a count of
forty the night the Fourth was doused, and paid for it with every memory of the boy.
His wife died a year later, worn out with being angry.
**Lie he believes:** *the lens is the last thing in this town that remembers what we
were* — as though a piece of glass could do the remembering for him.
**Arc:** he does not get his son back. He gets a name pasted above the bottle shelf and
reads it out loud every morning, which is not the same and is enough to keep going.
**Voice:** slow, dry, no self-pity at all, which is what makes it unbearable.
**Tag:** flour on his sleeves, a glass he dries instead of shouting, a framed deed by the
door with four signatures on it.
**Tell:** he says *lad*.

## Jory Beck — 20, and he knows

**Wants:** his father to look at him the way he looks at a stranger who has come in out
of the rain.
**Wound:** he remembers everything, and is the only person in Candlewick who does.
**Lie he believes:** *if I keep the bar clean and the rooms let, he'll come back.*
**Voice:** quick, obliging, careful with the word *Da*.
**Tell:** he sets the glass down before he answers.

## Warden Ansel Halloran — 63, head of the Emberwatch

**Wants:** to hand his order on intact to somebody who can live with what it has signed.
**Wound:** he signed the schedule eleven years ago — eight dousings and one deferral — and
has been re-signing the deferral for the Ninth every quarter since, in his own hand, which
he knows is not courage.
**Lie he believes:** *if I keep the institution alive, the institution will eventually be
worth what it costs.*
**Voice:** dry, exact, no self-pity; says the true arithmetic before anyone can accuse him of
something else. Calls everybody by their full name.
**Tag:** nine brass pins in a board — eight in the cork, one lying on its side; a window that
looks at the sea; the ninth pin of the stair door in a drawer.
**Arc:** he does not turn. He gives Mira the arithmetic in the first person, asks her to stop
being seen, gives her until Thursday, and then does the thing an institution does.
**Tell:** he says *which is a sentence I have had eleven years to think about.*

## Odo Pike — 62, guide-sergeant of the green bands

**Wants:** to hand eleven bands, and four hundred years of road, to somebody who will not sell
them.
**Wound:** he has lost about four years in pieces and cannot say which four; he keeps the
tally on the wall like a man in a cell.
**Lie he believes:** that the eleven are a remnant rather than a decision he keeps making.
**Voice:** flat, practical, a bit amused; answers a question by doing the thing; never
lectures and always finishes the sentence.
**Tag:** a forge-hand's coat, a rope burn healed badly, a band worn thin on the inside, boots
by the door.
**Tell:** he pushes the pot across the table with the back of his hand.
**Where he lands:** the guides get a coast moving at night in Part Four; the box on the wall
stays on the wall because he has stood in front of it at four in the morning and walked away.
See [[The Bands]].

## Rill — 14, the youngest band

**Wants:** to be given the long road instead of the short one.
**Wound:** he has never seen any of the country he knows in daylight.
**Voice:** direct, a little proud, explains by showing you.
**Tell:** he holds out his hand, palm up, like a boy who has been told the road is always
there.

## Marshal Hale — 50, field commander of the order

**Wants:** the gift of being wrong out loud in a building full of men who are certain.
**Wound:** transferred west three times for asking about the Eighth; she has stopped asking in
public and has not stopped keeping notes.
**Voice:** short sentences, no ceremony, arrives straight at the point.
**Tag:** grey, built like a door, four sergeants who move when she does.
**The yellow (Ch. 27):** she wears the watch band — the order's second instrument, three of them in
the fortress, the hours paid by a parish instead of a man. It shows who is on the road: the living
as warm shapes, the Hollowed as holes, the Ash as the place where the road stops being there. She
has been a watch sergeant nineteen years and says it is the only thing in the fortress she is
afraid of. In the council she puts it on, looks at nine hundred people walking south, and asks to
take it off.
**Tell:** she says *thank God* in a yard in front of a hundred people and does not care who
hears it.

## Ossian Vey — 30, licensed keeper of the Fifth

**Wants:** a coast where the light is regulated, licensed, and cannot be put out by a
captain with a glove.
**Wound:** he was holding the wick at the Fifth when the fleet doused it, and he has
been trying to make that never happen again by paperwork ever since.
**Lie he believes:** *a signature is the only thing that ever stopped anyone.*
**Arc:** he does not turn. He stays reasonable, correct and courteous to the last
page, and that is the point of him.
**Voice:** warm, precise, never raises it; offers you what you want and writes down
what you said.
**Tag:** a keeper's grey cut by a better tailor than the coast has; a ring of glass
over brass with a seam in it; a box of forms; a clerk.
**The secret:** he is the Crown's assessor. He has been reading the Roll's copy for
nine years and he knows things that were never written down.
**Tell:** he does not look at the light he is talking about. One other tell per
appearance, never two. Full note: [[The Assessor]].

## Ordinator Anselm Cray — 50, the Scarlet Assize

**Wants:** a coast hard enough to survive what is behind the Ash — and, underneath that, one
person to tell him it did not have to cost this much.
**Wound:** the wall at **Harrow**, nineteen years ago: four hundred people behind him, the thing
coming up the road in the shape of the people it had eaten, and a blade that held because eleven
men agreed to a bargain nobody explained to them.
**Lie he believes:** *a thing that survives is worth more than a thing that stays kind.*
**Voice:** a schoolmaster's — tired, courteous, precise, and never raised. He reads lists. He
apologises for the necessity and not for the act.
**Tag:** a plain grey coat with a red cord at the shoulder, and a folded list he does not need.
**The doctrine:** something worse than the Ash is coming over the Fall, and soft coasts are eaten
first; the Assize breaks soft coasts on purpose so they will be hard when it arrives. *We are not
the thing over the Fall. We are the tempering before it.*
**The one crack in him:** he made a **teal blade** for his novice out of seventeen seasons given
knowingly at Ostrey, and cannot say the word *superstition* without looking away. He left the boy
at the Kindling to *learn what a kept coast is*.
**Full codex:** [[The Scarlet Assize]].

## The Receiver — the Brine Mother's clerk

A woman of about forty in a grey coat that is not wet, with an old book and a clasp. She has read
the account out to coasts for four hundred years and does not blink. She is not cruel and cannot be
persuaded: *I do not choose. The Mother is the creditor.* Her one courtesy is that she reads the
whole account before she takes anything. Her one horror is that she keeps the collection moving at
the rate of the interest — one Hollowed over the lip at a time, from four in the afternoon, whether
or not the coast is ready. Full terms: [[The Reach]] → the Brine Mother.

## Toby Larch — 16, the Assize's novice

A boy on a bollard with a blade that has never lit, left behind on purpose by the only father he
has. Carries the **teal** blade — seventeen volunteered seasons, grey for four years — and it comes
up when Mira's ring comes near it, which is the first thing all day that gets brighter instead of
going out. He is the book's hope in object form and he does not know it. Rules: he is frightened,
not cute; he asks the questions the reader is asking; and the teal never cuts anything.

## Admiral Ilse Voss — 54, the Salt Throne

**Wants:** to keep the Ash off the coast, at any price, forever.
**Wound:** her brother **Konrad Voss**, keeper of the Second at Brinehollow, went
Hollowed the night the town went down, and she keeps him in a room with a window and
a chair and a chessboard. He beats her every night, because she asks him *which one?*
and moves the piece he names; she could win, and has never once moved his pieces badly.
He answers any question put to him and cannot ask one, and he has not said her name in
eleven years without being asked.
**The plan:** relight all nine inside eleven days, in sequence, under licence — a wall
of light the Throne holds. It is not stupid. It is the best plan anybody has, and it
requires a keeper whose light can be turned off.
**The hand:** the back of her right hand is scarred shiny from knuckles to wrist —
she pulled her brother out of the water at Brinehollow with it. Hence the white gloves,
indoors, always.
**Underneath:** she wants her brother to say her name without being asked. She has asked
him every night for eleven years at the end of the day, and it is the last question she
has left.
**Where she lands:** she does not change — she bends, which is not the same thing, and she
bends for arithmetic. She is proved half right, which is worse, and she dies having saved
more lives than Mira will ever know.
**Lie she believes:** *the dark keeps us safe, and safety is the only thing
anyone is owed.* (She is the book's thesis with the sign flipped, which is why
she is an antagonist and not a monster.)
**Arc:** she does not change. She is proved half right, which is worse, and she
dies having saved more lives than Mira will ever know.
**Voice:** precise, tired, never raises her voice, asks questions she already
knows the answers to.
**Tag:** a glass-sight she never looks through; salt-white gloves.
**Tell:** she calls everyone by their full name, including herself.
**The ship (Ch. 19):** the window was a beacon and they came to it. Her brother stands
up at the fifty-third stroke — the first time in eleven years — and says he is waiting
for the bell. Voss puts her nine hundred men into the boats, gives Mira the *Tongue*,
the lens and a receipt, and takes the *Vigil* out past the Cradle at four to put her on
the north rocks with fifty-six of the Ash's people aboard her and not one man of hers.
She says *good* when Mira tells her she did not sign the form.
**The order (Ch. 20):** eleven years ago she stood in Ede Calloway's kitchen in the snow,
with the scarred hand and a receipt, and told her the light was a door and the dark was a
harbour and that if the door was shut the child would live. Ede shut it. It is the only
order Voss ever gave that she has never put in a file, and she has never gone back to
Carrow to look at what it cost.
**The window (Ch. 18):** the only light on a ship with forty empty brass brackets.
She keeps it lit because a keeper trims a wick and her brother still does that much,
and because in the dark he is a coat on a hook. She knows exactly what a light nine
miles out on the edge of the Ash is worth, and says so out loud, once, to a girl:
*put it out and you tell me what is left of him.*

**The Thursday (Ch. 24):** the writ executed properly and in person. She lands with a case
holding three Embers — the Second, the Seventh, the Eighth — and says of the Eighth, in front
of the girl who held that tower: *the family are Hollowed and a Hollowed keeper cannot give a
season, which is a sentence no clerk in Saltmere wanted to write and I made him write it.*
She has five lanterns lit and calls it *what a formula gets you*, and she is neither proud
nor ashamed, which is worse.

She does not deny the stipend. She wrote the schedule in the second year and has signed every
quarter since, and her defence is the one nobody can answer: **it worked.** *Eight towns that
were going to be eaten were not eaten… I have never once been able to work out whether I am
the woman who saved the coast or the woman who sold it.* She says the second half of that
sentence to a girl of eighteen in a cold hall, because there is nobody else on the Reach she
can say it to.

The one illegal thing: she leaves the guides **off the Glass Roll** for nine days and gives a
keeper without a licence a **warrant of sufferance** she has no authority to issue, and she
knows precisely what it will cost her — *they will retire me. It's worse. A hanging is over
in an afternoon.* She does it for arithmetic, not for mercy, and the chapter should never let
the reader forget the difference: a fleet was held hostage by one small boat, and the woman
who folds is the woman who counts.

Her quarters carry the chessboard. She has never once been allowed to play black.

## Ede Calloway — 71, the last keeper of the Ninth

**Wants:** to keep the Ash out of the Reach for one more winter.
**Wound:** she has been holding the island every night for eleven years with no
light in her hands at all, one season at a time, and she has spent almost all of
herself on it.
**Lie she believes:** *if I stop, everything I have forgotten was wasted.*
**Arc:** found, not rescued. She does not know her granddaughter's face; she has
the name, at the top of the page, every morning, and cannot say whose it is. She
gets one clean sentence at the end and makes it count.
**Tag:** matches in every pocket — she touches them all, counting, every ninth or
tenth step; a keeper's book of fifty names written every morning; a ring with
nothing in it; a man's coat over two jerseys.
**Tell:** she asks the date, and then writes down the answer.
**The wall (Ch. 20):** she holds a **ring** round the whole island at the waterline —
not a lane, not a bar, a closed shape with no ends to walk round. It has no colour,
which is the whole trick: a bright edge can be studied, so the Ash comes up the road,
finds a door it cannot see the end of, and goes home. Cost: a season a year, and she
has had eleven. She stands in a dish worn in the stone by eleven years of standing,
and the sea inside the ring lies down. She has not been asked a question by anyone in
Carrow in eleven years, because a parish will feed a mad woman and will not ask her
anything. Full note: [[The Light of Will]].
**The page (Ch. 20):** *Third of the eleventh. Snow. I have put it out.* — and under
it, in her own careful hand, the woman with the bad hand, the light as a door, the
dark as a harbour, and *I asked her what becomes of the child. She said: nothing, if
the door is shut.* She does not remember writing it and reads it like a stranger's
letter. It is the answer to the Admiral's question, and the Admiral has never come to
look at the woman standing on the wall.
**The lamp room (Ch. 21):** she has climbed the hundred and twelve steps every week for
 eleven years and kept the tower *ready*, not clean — brass polished, wick trimmed, a
 cushion worn through in the chair — and the log has five hundred and seventy entries in
 one hand and one sentence: *dark, wick trimmed, nothing to report.* She sits in the chair
 and has Mira read the names out so she can say them back. She kept her daughter written
 four hundred times and crossed out and written again.
**The page (Ch. 21):** she tears the last page out of the book and gives it to Mira — the
 page with **Tess Calloway** on it — because *you can still learn a name and mean it, so
 learn it.* Mira spends it that same evening holding a lane.
**The ring (Ch. 21):** she gives her dead keeper's ring to Rook, who puts it on his right
 thumb in place of the iron and carries the iron in his left hand. *I keep no light of my
 own.* / *I know. That's why you get the empty one.*
**The word (Ch. 21):** Mira has called her Ede all day, which is a keeper's name. At the
 slip she says *grandmother*, twice, and Ede writes it down and says she will say it in
 the morning, with the names. It is the last thing either of them says to the other before
 the wall.
**What she left:** the vow on the inside cover of the notebook — she pasted the
Crown's version over the old one and then pasted the old one back on top, badly,
which is the bravest thing she does in the book and nobody sees it.

## Tess Calloway — 29 when she died, Mira's mother

**Wanted:** not recorded anywhere, by anyone.
**What happened:** she went into the water at Carrow the night the lights went out. Her
mother has the date and the fact and no face; her daughter had the name for twenty
minutes in Chapter Twenty-One and spent it on a Holding. The parish will not talk about
her in a way that adds up, and the one person who kept asking is the one the light keeps
charging.
**Function:** the proof that the book's accounting is not fair. Tess is not a mystery to
be solved in Part Four; she is a name on a torn page in a coat pocket, and the reader
should feel the weight of a person who exists only as handwriting.

## Ossa Tarn — 60, the chandlery at Carrow

**Wants:** nobody to say it out loud, in case it hears.
**Wound:** eleven years of baking a loaf for a woman who will not eat it, and never
once asking why nothing comes ashore.
**Voice:** flat, practical, no fuss; runs the parish without holding any office in it.
**Tag:** a man's jersey with the sleeves rolled; a lamp turned down low in the doorway.
**Tell:** she answers a question with the parish's answer, and the parish's answer is
*we don't ask.*

## Ossa's parish

Four hundred people, one hill, one dark tower, and one arrangement nobody has ever
looked at: the coast that the Ash has never touched is the coast where a woman walks
the wall every night, and the parish has decided that this is luck, or the saint, or
the tide.

## The Brine Mother

Not a person. A contract with a voice. Appears as a woman at the foot of the
Fall, made of rope and current and something that has been under the water a long
time. She is scrupulously fair, which is not the same as kind, and she collects
in units of light. Her price for the *Mercy Bell* was "the light of a lantern,
freely given" — and she will not accept a stolen one, which is Isolde's entire
problem.

## The Hollowed

People the Ash has emptied. They keep their jobs, their manners and their faces.
They cannot initiate anything — not a sentence, not a step, not a meal. Put a
question to one and they will answer it. Leave one alone in a room and it will
stand there until somebody comes back.

Voss's brother **Konrad** is Hollowed and plays chess by naming the piece he wants
and letting her move it, and beats her every night, and cannot ask her a question.
This is the most frightening thing in the book.

## Relationships, quickly

- **Mira ↔ Teo:** the spine. Two people who are good at being needed and bad at
  being wanted.
- **Mira ↔ Isolde:** the daughter-shaped hole in a captain who has never kept
  anything she couldn't sell.
- **Mira ↔ Rook:** the mirror. Both obey a dead woman's instructions; only one of
  them learns to stop.
- **Isolde ↔ Rook:** mutual professional disgust that becomes the best friendship
  in the book.
- **Voss ↔ Ede:** the argument the book is actually having, conducted across
  eleven years and eight dark towers.

## Romance beats (Mira and Teo)

Keep these small. The big declarations are cheap; the logistics are not.
**The counter-vow** (Ch. 6) ✔ — *if I forget you* / *then I'll introduce myself
again.* Said once, at the bow, with a notebook open between them, and paid in
Chapter Thirty. See [[The Two of Them]].

1. **The knife and the biscuit** (Ch. 6) ✔ — he gives her the good knife and the
   worse half of the food and refuses to make it mean anything.
2. **The name in the notebook** (Ch. 6–7) — she writes his name down where he can
   see it, because she writes everything down, and adds *he stays* under it. In
   Ch. 7 there is a new line in the book, in his handwriting, and she does not
   mention it and neither does he.
3. **The watch** (Ch. 10) — the two-hour rotation where neither of them sleeps
   and they talk about nothing in particular, and it is the best night of the
   book.
4. **The lie in the light** (Ch. 16) ✔ — his Holding fails because he lies to her
   in lantern light, and she learns he has been lying about something small. The
   big true thing holds; the kindness after it does not, and the lens takes his
   sight of the revealed world for it.
5. **The rescue** (Ch. 24) — he comes back for her when running would have been
   the sensible thing. It is the first thing he has ever done that he cannot
   explain as a transaction.
6. **The remembering** (Ch. 30) — she has spent herself; he has been writing all
   along. The book ends with him reading her back to herself: *if I forget you* /
   *then I'll introduce myself again*, paid in full.
