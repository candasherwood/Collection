# The Lantern's Edge — Story Bible

*A keeper's granddaughter lights a dead lighthouse and learns that the world is
the broken rim of a ring, that light is will made visible, and that everything
hungry in the dark has been waiting eleven years for somebody to be brave in
public.*

This folder is the whole project: the manuscript chapters sit at the top level
and everything I need to keep them honest lives in `Bible/`.

## What the book is about

Not "light versus dark". That fight is already lost; the dark is patient and it
does not hate anyone. The question is whether a light is worth showing when
showing it tells the dark exactly where you are.

Eleven years ago the Salt Throne put out eight of the nine lanterns to starve the
Ash. It worked. It also cost the Reach its roads, its history, its safe harbours
and, in the end, most of what its keepers could remember. Mira's generation grew
up in the dark and calls it peace.

Mira lights the Ninth because her grandmother's notebook told her to. What she
learns, over thirty chapters, is that the light is not a weapon and not a
promise. It is a *witness*: it shows what is there, including you, including the
people you would rather not be seen by.

## Rules that do not bend

1. **The light takes memory.** Every Holding burns something the keeper
   remembers. This is the engine of the whole book — see [[The Light of Will]].
2. **No prophecy, no chosen one.** Mira is chosen by nobody. Her ring fits
   because her grandmother's did, and that is all.
3. **The Ash cannot be fought.** It can be kept out, outrun, or starved. Nobody
   defeats it in a duel.
4. **Light reveals; it does not create truth.** It shows what is already there,
   which is usually worse.
5. **The sea is not a backdrop.** Weather, tide, salt and cold get a vote in
   every plan.

## House style

- Numbers, objects and weather instead of adjectives. "Eleven years." "One
  hundred and twelve steps." "A box of matches, label to the door."
- Short paragraphs at moments of pressure. Longer ones when Mira is thinking and
  pretending she isn't.
- Humour is carried by Isolde (dry, ruinous) and Teo (fast, deflecting).
- Nobody explains the world. Mira was raised in it; she notices, she doesn't
  lecture.
- Keep a running note of **what Mira has forgotten**. It is a continuity
  requirement, not a flourish — see the list at the bottom of [[Outline]].
